How it works

A weather report for the parts of life that run through government.

Put in a ZIP and you get what's coming where you live: when and how to vote, what's on the ballot, what rent, groceries, gas and jobs are doing, and the dates that touch most households. Here's where all of it comes from.

Voting rules

Every state's rules (early voting dates, how mail ballots have to come back, whether you can register on Election Day, poll hours, and the official lookup page) were researched on October 26, 2026 from that state's own election office: its website, its election calendar, or its official voter guide. We didn't use third-party summaries as a source for any of them.

Where a state's own pages disagree, or a court order changes a deadline, the forecast shows the stricter date (the one that can't cost you your vote) and explains the conflict under "Good to know." A handful of states are marked with a note asking you to double-check, because a detail couldn't be fully confirmed.

Rules can change late, especially through court rulings. Your state election office is always the final word, and every state's forecast links straight to it.

What's on the ballot

The forecast shows the statewide federal and governor's races: all 435 U.S. House seats, 35 U.S. Senate races (including special elections in Florida and Ohio), and 36 governor's races, checked against senate.gov and state election offices. It doesn't list candidates or your local races, because those depend on your exact address. Your state's lookup page has your full ballot.

Where a race has a debate, it links to Same Question, where you read what the candidates actually said with their names hidden, then see who said what. Every quote there was checked against the debate audio.

Life where you live

  • Rent: the Zillow Observed Rent Index, the typical asking rent for homes and apartments. We use your ZIP code when Zillow tracks it (about 6,200 do), otherwise your county, otherwise your state. State figures are the median across the ZIPs Zillow tracks there, which leans toward cities. Updated monthly.
  • Groceries, gas, electricity: the Bureau of Labor Statistics Consumer Price Index for your Census region (Northeast, Midwest, South, West), compared with the same month a year earlier. Updated monthly.
  • Jobs: your state's unemployment rate from the Bureau of Labor Statistics, seasonally adjusted. Updated monthly.
  • Air: the current U.S. Air Quality Index for your location from Open-Meteo, read on the EPA's scale. Live.

Alerts

Alerts work like severe-weather warnings, most urgent first. "Act soon" means a deadline is close. "Heads up" means a deadline is coming. "Good to know" is useful but not urgent. "Warning" is reserved for things like a severe weather warning or unhealthy air.

Voting alerts come from your state's rules and today's date. Weather alerts come live from the National Weather Service for your exact location. Disaster alerts come from FEMA's public declarations data and only appear when a declaration from the last 90 days covers your county. We only say you may be able to apply for help when the declaration includes help for individuals.

The calendar

The dates beyond voting (Medicare and HealthCare.gov enrollment, Social Security and SSI changes, tax deadlines, federal holidays, the clocks changing) come from the agencies that run them: Medicare.gov, HealthCare.gov and CMS, Social Security, the IRS, and the Office of Personnel Management. Dates that hadn't been officially announced yet, like when the IRS starts taking 2026 returns, are left out until they are.

The map

Each layer colors the states by one thing: how people can vote today, where you can still register, how rent moved in a year, and unemployment. The colors were checked so people with common kinds of color blindness can tell them apart, and the list view shows every state in words.

Your places, plan and reminders

The places you save, the voting plan you pick, and whether you've voted are kept only in this browser. They never leave it, and we have no copy.

"Add to calendar" and "Remind me" make a standard calendar file with your dates and a reminder the evening before. Your phone's calendar keeps it and does the reminding, so we don't need to know who you are or how to reach you.

The "I voted" sticker is drawn in your browser. It shows your state and the year, never who you voted for.

What it doesn't do

  • It never tells you who to vote for, or how. It doesn't rank or rate candidates.
  • It doesn't know your precinct, your polling place, or your exact ballot. It sends you to your state for those.
  • It has no accounts, ads, or analytics. Your ZIP is saved only in your browser. To build your forecast, the page looks it up with free public services (Zippopotam.us and the FCC to find your place, Open-Meteo for weather and air, the National Weather Service, and FEMA). It doesn't send your ZIP anywhere else, and we don't keep it.
  • County-level differences (early voting hours, drop box locations) aren't covered. Where they matter, the forecast says so.
